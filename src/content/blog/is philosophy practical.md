for context, i've been thinking about how people treat their freedoms (or lack of)? and how most are fine with it. you use chrome, google syncs all your data and emails, every login is via google. great! i dont have to remember my passwords as much, it's convenient... right?

99% of the world just runs like that laissez-faire and the only thing keeping it together is Trust that google keeps sucking your data from you to show ads "personalised to your taste" as if that's not a Little Bit Unsettling to say the least. but I'm personally guilty of this because i've just never gotten bitten in the ass for it. i reuse the same passwords everywhere, i sync most of my bookmarks and accounts through google, and it's still been fine. convenience for the low price of your privacy

flip to the other side, and there are levels as to like how much someone values their autonomy. you have the people who just like to think about it as a passing thought. Yeah i use Brave and Bitwarden. Ignore that IME backdoor. Windows? That's fine, right? i need it to play my Hit Game Valorant and run kernel level anticheat doing god knows what spelunking through your memory. And god forbid you try to have a window called x64dbg open, nonono you don't get to have windows we don't like open here! this is still OK, but obviously like. a little bit doublethink-like? you think you want privacy but you only do the bare minimum for it and don't bother learning or sacrificing some of the "easiness" in your life

fine. I'll use Linux! it's FOSS and nothing could go wro- wait... supply chains?? what the fuck is that? everyone just installs packages and hopes that no dependencies get compromised? that's crazy! eh but Open Source means that people will be looking and combing over code for vulnerabilities... right? anyway . philosophy of how you value your freedom is really interesting because of this. how do people choose how much they want to give up..? VPNs and rooting your phone, setting up OPNSense, hell if you don't trust your ISP or Starlink (I wouldn't either tbh) how the fuck are you getting internet? ~~be like those bums in starbucks hogging seats?~~ 

this analogy doesn't extend to just privacy, but to just how you treat your life in general. the purpose of a system is what it does, not what it's intended to do. just because I tell myself everyday that "Yeah I'm totally gonna wake up and work on that and disappoint myself with all those inflated expectations at 3am with a sudden surge of motivation" doesn't mean I'll be continuing to do that. and How Ironic it was that I myself didn't realise it until my friend told me.
